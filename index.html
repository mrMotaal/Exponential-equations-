The Fundamental Laws of Exponential Equations

In standard polynomial equations, the variable is in the base (e.g., $x^2 = 9$). In Exponential Equations, the unknown variable is in the power (e.g., $3^x = 81$). Here are the golden rules taught by Mr Ahmed Abd El-Motaal.

1 Base Equality

Equal Bases $\implies$ Equal Powers

If $a^x = a^y$ where $a \notin \{ -1, 0, 1 \}$, then the powers must be equal:

$$a^x = a^y \implies x = y$$
Example: $2^{x-7} = 2^{-5} \implies x = 2$
2 Equal Powers

Equal Powers $\implies$ Base Equivalence or Power = 0

If $a^m = b^m$, then:

$\bullet$ If $m$ is odd $\implies a = b$
$\bullet$ If $m$ is even $\implies a = \pm b$
$\bullet$ If $a \neq b \implies m = 0$
Example: $2^{x+1} = 3^{x+1} \implies x = -1$
3 RHS = 1

Right Hand Side is 1 $\implies$ Power is Zero

Since any non-zero real number raised to the zero power equals 1 ($a^0 = 1$):

$$a^{f(x)} = 1 \implies f(x) = 0 \quad (a \neq 0)$$
Example: $4^{x^2 - 9} = 1 \implies x = \pm 3$

Prerequisite Refresh: Fundamental Laws of Powers

Essential Toolkit
1 Product Law
$$a^m \times a^n = a^{m+n}$$

Keep the common base and add the powers together.

2 Quotient Law
$$\frac{a^m}{a^n} = a^{m-n} \quad (a \neq 0)$$

Keep the common base and subtract the powers.

3 Power of a Power
$$(a^m)^n = a^{m \cdot n}$$

Multiply the inner and outer powers directly.

4 Negative Power Law
$$a^{-n} = \frac{1}{a^n} \quad (a \neq 0)$$

A negative power is the reciprocal of the positive power.

5 Fraction Inversion Law
$$\left(\frac{a}{b}\right)^{-n} = \left(\frac{b}{a}\right)^n$$

Inverting the fraction changes the sign of the power.

6 Zero Power & Distribution
$$a^0 = 1 \quad \text{and} \quad (ab)^n = a^n b^n$$

Any non-zero base to power 0 is 1. Powers distribute over factors.

⚡ Mr Ahmed Abd El-Motaal's 6 Key Exam Hacks

1. Factor Tree Always

Always break large composite numbers ($81, 32, 625, 125$) into prime base powers ($3^4, 2^5, 5^4, 5^3$).

2. Power of a Power Distribution

Remember $(a^m)^n = a^{m \cdot n}$. Distribute the inner power to ALL terms of a binomial power!

3. GCF Factor Extraction

When terms are added (e.g. $2^x + 2^{x+1} + 2^{x+2}$), pull out the smallest term $2^x$ as a common factor!

4. Chain Power Substitution

If $2^a = 27 = 3^3$ and $2^b = 3$, substitute $3 = 2^b$ directly to get $2^a = (2^b)^3 = 2^{3b}$.

5. Quadratic Power Factoring

If $2^{x^2} = 16^{3-x}$, simplify to a standard quadratic $x^2 + 4x - 12 = 0$ and solve for both roots!

6. Parity Verification

For even powers like $(x-1)^4 = 16$, always include both $\pm 2$ roots in your final solution set!
