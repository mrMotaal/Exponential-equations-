Mastering Exponential Equations & Exponent Rules

An Exponential Equation is an equation where the unknown variable sits in the exponent (power), rather than in the base. Solving these equations is governed by 3 fundamental mathematical laws and systematic prime base reduction.

Essential Definition Contrast:

Polynomial Equation $x^2 = 16 \implies x = \pm 4$
(Variable is in base)
Exponential Equation $2^x = 16 \implies 2^x = 2^4 \implies x = 4$
(Variable is in exponent)
$$a^x = a^y \implies x = y \quad (a \notin \{-1, 0, 1\})$$

The Three Core Principles (From Lecture):

1
Equal Bases: When $a^x = a^y$ and $a \notin \{-1, 0, 1\}$, powers equate: $x = y$.
2
Unit RHS ($= 1$): When $a^P = 1$ ($a \ne 0$), the exponent must be zero: $P = 0$ (since $a^0 = 1$).
3
Different Bases with Equal Exponent: When $a^P = b^P$ where $a \ne b$ (e.g. $2^{x+1} = 5^{x+1}$), the exponent must be zero: $P = 0$.
Base Equivalence Engine
$a^x = b$ $a^x = a^k \implies x = k$
$$2^{x-7} = \frac{1}{32} = 2^{-5} \implies x - 7 = -5 \implies x = 2$$
Pro Tip: Always memorize the prime powers: $2^1$ to $2^7$, $3^1$ to $3^5$, and $5^1$ to $5^4$.

Tips, Tricks & Exam Hacks

6 Secret Strategies
Hack #1

The Prime Decomposition Tree

When encountering composite numbers, break them down immediately:

$81 = 3^4 \quad|\quad 27 = 3^3$
$32 = 2^5 \quad|\quad 64 = 2^6 \quad|\quad 128 = 2^7$
$125 = 5^3 \quad|\quad 625 = 5^4$

Never leave $9, 16, 27, 81$ unfactored.

Hack #2

Fractional Flipping Shortcut

Flipping a fraction negates the entire exponent. If bases are reciprocal fractions, flip one side and change all signs:

$\frac{1}{a^n} = a^{-n}$
$\left(\frac{2}{3}\right)^P = \left(\frac{3}{2}\right)^{-P}$
$\left(\frac{4}{9}\right) = \left(\frac{2}{3}\right)^2 \implies \left(\frac{4}{9}\right)^x = \left(\frac{3}{2}\right)^{-2x}$

Watch out: flip reverses signs of all terms in exponent.

Hack #3

The Right-Hand $= 1$ "Gift"

Mr Ahmed calls this "The Gift" in the video. Whenever the right-hand side is $1$, you do not need to factor anything!

$a^{f(x)} = 1 \implies f(x) = 0$
$3^{x^2 - 9} = 1 \implies x^2 - 9 = 0 \implies x = \pm 3$
$7^{2x - 6} = 1 \implies 2x - 6 = 0 \implies x = 3$

Trap alert: $x^2 = 9$ gives $\pm 3$, do not forget the negative value!

Hack #4

Splitting & Common Factoring

When terms are added, e.g. $2^x + 2^{x+1}$, you CANNOT add the exponents! Split and factor:

$2^{x+1} = 2^x \cdot 2^1 \quad|\quad 2^{x+2} = 2^x \cdot 2^2$
$2^x(1 + 2 + 4) = 112$
$7 \cdot 2^x = 112 \implies 2^x = 16 \implies x = 4$

Never write $2^x + 2^{x+1} = 2^{2x+1}$ (huge error!).

Hack #5

Quadratic Exponent Rearrangement

When powers result in an equation with $x^2$ and $x$, rearrange to $Ax^2 + Bx + C = 0$:

$2^{x^2} = 16^{3-x} \implies x^2 = 4(3-x)$
$x^2 + 4x - 12 = 0$
$(x + 6)(x - 2) = 0 \implies x \in \{2, -6\}$

Use calculator `MODE 5 3` to verify factors immediately.

Hack #6

Chain Exponential Substitution

When given two equations with different powers (e.g. $2^a = 27$ and $2^b = 3$):

$27 = 3^3 = (2^b)^3 = 2^{3b}$
Since $2^a = 2^{3b} \implies a = 3b$
Then $\frac{a+b}{b} = \frac{3b+b}{b} = \frac{4b}{b} = 4$

Express the larger composite in terms of the smaller base.

Laws of Exponents Quick Reference

Law Name Algebraic Formula Practical Application Frequent Mistake to Avoid
Product Law $a^m \cdot a^n = a^{m+n}$ $2^x \cdot 2^3 = 2^{x+3}$ Multiplying bases: $2^x \cdot 2^3 \ne 4^{x+3}$
Quotient Law $\frac{a^m}{a^n} = a^{m-n}$ $\frac{3^{2x+1}}{3^x} = 3^{x+1}$ Dividing exponents instead of subtracting
Power of a Power $(a^m)^n = a^{m \cdot n}$ $(5^2)^{x+1} = 5^{2x+2}$ Forgetting to distribute: $5^{2x+1}$ (forgetting $2 \times 1$)
Zero Exponent $a^0 = 1 \quad (a \ne 0)$ $3^{x-4} = 1 \implies x - 4 = 0$ Thinking $a^0 = 0$
Negative Exponent $a^{-n} = \frac{1}{a^n}$ $\frac{1}{32} = 2^{-5}$ Assuming negative power makes the number negative
